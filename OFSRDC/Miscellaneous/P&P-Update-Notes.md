# P&P Update Notes

Created: 2009-01-09 17:51:14 -0800

Modified: 2009-02-02 22:48:07 -0800

---

-   I don't remember anyone complaining about the length of the P&Ps.
-   Let us remember the purposes of the P&Ps. The P&Ps are there to make our jobs easier, by acting as a repository for the kinds of helpful information that people forget to pass on. They help incoming officers to know what they should be doing and when, and to help potential officers know what they are signing up for. Sections should be removed when they are out of date or misleading. Lacking that, it seems to me that the more guidance we can provide, the better.
-   These same comments apply to the sample letters. It isn't important whether the names on the letters are up-to-date or out of date, the key is that the letters be there to help an incoming officer who needs to get a job done.
-   Some of the information in the P&Ps was clearly of historical interest only, but it seems unfortunate to remove it without having some plan for its archival. The unfortunate fire and subsequent disappearance of the Historian demonstrates how much our history is at risk.
-   This change doubles the mileage and hotel reimbursement for meetings. That's a major financial commitment, and cannot be done as part of a bulk procedure update.
-   The P&Ps are supposed to embody the way things are really done. The new requirement that the parliamentarian be "accredited" violates that rule, and borders on unethical.
-   Our by-laws make Robert's Rules our parliamentary authority. Mr. Bosch has extended this to imply that the whole of the Rules are included as PART of our by-laws. This is imaginative nonsense. Rules are used where they are not in conflict with our own rules, and Robert's Rules themselves repeatedly assert that they are guidelines to be used when an organization's own policies do not cover certain situations.
