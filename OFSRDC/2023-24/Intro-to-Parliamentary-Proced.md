**Introduction to Parliamentary Procedure**



-   Background reference: Robert's Rules of Order
-   A way to allow everyone to have a say on controversial topics, while still providing a way to conduct ordinary business efficiently
-   Chairman has ultimate power
    -   *Expected to proceed according to Robert, but in reality can do anything as long as the body does not object*
    -   *But cannot make motions unless he gives up the chair...*
    -   *And does not vote except to break or make a tie*
-   Basic way of doing business is through the motion
    -   *Make them during new business or during your report*
    -   *One issue at a time is discussed and disposed of before moving on*
    -   *Mechanism:*
        -   "Madame Chairman, I move that ...."
        -   "Is there a second?"
        -   "I second the motion"; no second means motion fails
        -   Chairman calls for debate or discussion
        -   Wait to be recognized and to get a microphone before speaking
        -   Identify yourself
        -   Keep to the issue
        -   You may move to amend the motion
        -   Amendments also need a second, a debate, and a vote
        -   After amendments are approved, the motion as amended is read and put to a vote
        -   Chairman is expected to watch for debate to die out
    -   *Delegates and elected officers may vote*
    -   *If more than half vote "aye", motion passes*
    -   *A tie means the motion fails:*
    -   *In case of a tie, chairman may vote to break the tie*
    -   *In case of a near-tie, chairman may vote to make a tie and thereby fail the motion*
    -   *Ways to dispose of a motion:*
        -   Objection before debate begins; rarely used
        -   Vote
        -   Refer to committee (specify existing or new committee)
        -   Postpone to a specific future date
            -   Appears as "old business" at that date; does not need to be re-moved
        -   Postpone indefinitely (kills the motion without a vote)
        -   Lay on the table ("table the motion")
            -   Misused to mean same thing as "postpone"
            -   Actually is another means to kill the motion
            -   Common usage probably cannot be stopped now
        -   Withdrawal by maker
    -   You will hear people call out "Question" as the debate winds down. Intent is to "Call for the question", to ask for an end to the debate and to hasten the vote. This is erroneous. "Call for the question" is a formal parliamentary move to immediately terminate debate, but it is a privileged motion that requires a 2/3 vote. (Senate doesn't have it; house does.) It is the chairman's job to judge when debate has ended and call for the vote; "question" is impolite.
-   PLEASE participate. That's why you're here.
    -   *You CAN make motions*
    -   *You SHOULD vote*
    -   *You MAY speak on any debate*
    -   *If you are not ready to vote on an issue, move to postpone*
